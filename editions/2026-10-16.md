- **Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company Q3 2026 results [CHOSEN]** — Reported 15 Oct 2026 via TSMC's Form 6-K filed with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission. Consolidated revenue of NT$1,494.24 billion (US$46.72 billion), up 50.9% year-over-year in NT$ / 41.2% in US$ terms and 16.2% quarter-over-quarter; net income NT$763.06 billion (+68.7% YoY); diluted EPS NT$29.43 (US$4.60 per ADR unit); gross margin 66.4%, operating margin 58.7%, net margin 51.1%. Advanced nodes (7nm and below) were 79% of wafer revenue; 2nm reached 8%. CFO Wendell Huang credited "strong demand for our leading-edge process technologies" and guided Q4 revenue to US$52.3B–54.3B at 65–67% gross margin. ### Pattern context
This week's editions tracked every layer of the AI build-out's plumbing: who might own the labs (Nvidia weighing a buy of Reflection AI, 12 Oct), who makes the silicon (Microsoft's in-house Maia 300, 13 Oct), whose numbers are real (Anthropic showing Q3 financials ahead of a record IPO, 14 Oct), and who supplies the capital (Japan funding US$21B of GlobalFoundries fabs, 15 Oct). Friday's TSMC blowout is the audited bottom of that stack: the foundry behind essentially every AI accelerator posted record revenue and guided higher still. The recurring 2026 throughline — that AI demand keeps pulling in ever-larger pools of capital — gets, for once, a fully verifiable datapoint, even as the demand side (private marks, record IPOs, credit stress at CoreWeave) stays harder to prove.
