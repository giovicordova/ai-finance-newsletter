### Stories considered
- **Bank of England Financial Stability Report flags AI data-centre debt (published 26 Nov 2026) [CHOSEN].** In its latest Financial Stability Report, the Bank of England's Financial Policy Committee said companies building AI data centres are on course to issue more than $305 billion of debt this year, and that the sector's broader borrowing now exceeds the annual borrowing of most governments, including Britain's. It described the financing as having grown "increasingly complex, circular and opaque," warning that stress in one area could more easily propagate to another; it also judged equity valuations "stretched" and dependent on AI delivering expected profits. ### Pattern context
Today's beat closes the week where it should: after five weeks of editions tracing *how* the AI buildout is being paid for — the demand prints (Nvidia 18 Nov), the record IPO (19–20 Nov), the >8% SpaceX chip-financing bonds (23 Nov), the 18-year Applied Digital lease (25 Nov) and Sierra's venture round (26 Nov) — a central bank has now put an official number and name on the structure. The Bank of England's >$305B data-centre-debt estimate and its "increasingly complex, circular and opaque" language are the regulator's version of the circularity motif these editions keep flagging (Nvidia on nearly every side of the deals funding its own chips). The news post stays strictly to the Bank's published facts; the directional read — stretched valuations, a souring of sentiment — is left to the labelled Friday take.

### Weekly threads (Friday only)
- **The rising cost and engineering of AI debt** — the week opened with SpaceX borrowing $32B at above 8% through an off-balance-sheet vehicle to fund Nvidia chips ([23 Nov](https://github.com/giovicordova/ai-finance-newsletter/blob/main/editions/2026-11-23.md)) and closed with the Bank of England estimating >$305B of AI-data-centre debt this year and calling the financing "complex, circular and opaque" ([27 Nov](https://github.com/giovicordova/ai-finance-newsletter/blob/main/editions/2026-11-27.md)). The question moved from *is demand real* to *how is it financed, and at what risk*.
- **Long-dated, take-or-pay data-centre commitments keep stacking** — Applied Digital signed a new 18-year, ~$5.4B lease to an investment-grade cloud tenant, pushing its contracted book past $41B ([25 Nov](https://github.com/giovicordova/ai-finance-newsletter/blob/main/editions/2026-11-25.md)). These multi-year offtake contracts are the collateral underpinning the debt the Bank of England is now scrutinising.
- **Private capital still accelerating while public AI equities wobble** — Sierra's $1.75B raise doubled its valuation to $30B on ~$400M of recurring revenue ([26 Nov](https://github.com/giovicordova/ai-finance-newsletter/blob/main/editions/2026-11-26.md)), and Nvidia's board restored founder Jensen Huang as chief executive ([24 Nov](https://github.com/giovicordova/ai-finance-newsletter/blob/main/editions/2026-11-24.md)) — even as Anthropic slipped below its $83 IPO price in a broad AI-stock pullback. Valuations in private markets and doubt in public ones are tightening at the same time.
