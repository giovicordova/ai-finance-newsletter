### Pattern context
All week the newsletter tracked private capital and debt flooding the AI-chip buildout at ever-richer marks: Broadcom lending Anthropic up to $42B via convertible notes (5 Oct), Cerebras's value swinging on OpenAI workload allocation (6 Oct), Etched fielding offers at $40–50B — double its two-month-old $21B mark (7 Oct), and SpaceX lining up ~$40B of GPU-collateralised borrowing to buy Nvidia chips (8 Oct). Today is the mirror image: the public market balking. Firmus — itself Nvidia/Blackstone/Jane Street-backed — pulled a ~$5B IPO because the ~$30.6B price (triple its August private mark) couldn't clear, the second data-centre listing to falter in three weeks. The thread that tightened all week: private valuations are compounding far faster than public buyers will validate, and the gap is now visible.

### Weekly threads (Friday only)
- **Debt is becoming the default tool of the chip buildout** — Broadcom agreed to lend Anthropic up to $42B via convertible notes, the supplier financing its own customer ([2026-10-05](https://github.com/giovicordova/ai-finance-newsletter/blob/main/editions/2026-10-05.md)); SpaceX lined up ~$40B of borrowing, collateralised by the Nvidia GPUs themselves, through the Apollo-led machinery Nvidia stood up in August ([2026-10-08](https://github.com/giovicordova/ai-finance-newsletter/blob/main/editions/2026-10-08.md)). The buildout is increasingly funded on borrowed money secured by the chips.
- **Private chip valuations are compounding violently** — Etched fielded offers at $40–50B, roughly double its $21B mark from two months earlier and ~8x its June level ([2026-10-07](https://github.com/giovicordova/ai-finance-newsletter/blob/main/editions/2026-10-07.md)), while newly public Cerebras swung 9–20% in a week on which OpenAI workload lands on which chip ([2026-10-06](https://github.com/giovicordova/ai-finance-newsletter/blob/main/editions/2026-10-06.md)). Marks are moving faster than deployment can justify.
- **The public market started pushing back** — Firmus pulled a ~$5B IPO after its ~$30.6B target (triple its August private mark) met weak demand, the second data-centre listing to falter in three weeks ([2026-10-09](https://github.com/giovicordova/ai-finance-newsletter/blob/main/editions/2026-10-09.md)). The week's private exuberance ran into a public market that wouldn't ratify the price.
