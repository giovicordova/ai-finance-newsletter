### Stories considered
- **Oracle fiscal Q1 2027 results (CHOSEN)** — Reported after the US market close on 10 Sep. Total revenue $19.3B, up 30% year-over-year; cloud infrastructure (IaaS) revenue up 121% to $7.4B; total cloud revenue (IaaS + SaaS) up 62% to $11.6B; non-GAAP EPS $1.92 (beat ~$1.73 consensus). Remaining performance obligations — the backlog of signed but not-yet-delivered contract revenue — reached $664 billion, up $209 billion year-over-year (from $455 billion a year earlier), after Oracle booked more than $30 billion of additional AI cloud contracts in the quarter. Management raised the full-year FY2027 revenue outlook to a minimum of $90 billion. Chosen as the freshest, largest, cleanly-dated money mover of the cycle: it was the after-close beat flagged in yesterday's Notes, and the $664B backlog is the demand-visibility capstone to a week that was otherwise about how the compute buildout is financed and supplied. Linked to Oracle's own investor-relations press release (primary source, allowlisted, free). ### Pattern context
This closes a week whose spine was the financing and supply of the AI-compute buildout: Nscale's $3.5B pre-IPO raise (7 Sep), Jane Street's ~$13B Crusoe compute lease (8 Sep), the Qualcomm–Amazon custom-chip warrant (9 Sep), and Harvey's $15.5B application-layer round (10 Sep). Oracle's $664B backlog is the demand-visibility capstone: it puts a hard number on how far forward the buildout's contracted revenue now stretches — and, per this week's previews, on how concentrated it is (roughly half tied to OpenAI). The after-close beat was pre-flagged in yesterday's Notes as the story to watch.

### Weekly threads (Friday only)
- **Compute underwritten by forward contracts** — the buildout is increasingly financed by multi-year signed commitments rather than spot demand: Nscale's ~$103B contracted backlog and $3.5B pre-IPO raise ([7 Sep](https://github.com/giovicordova/ai-finance-newsletter/blob/main/editions/2026-09-07.md)), Jane Street's ~$13B five-year Crusoe lease ([8 Sep](https://github.com/giovicordova/ai-finance-newsletter/blob/main/editions/2026-09-08.md)), and Oracle's $664B remaining performance obligations ([11 Sep](https://github.com/giovicordova/ai-finance-newsletter/blob/main/editions/2026-09-11.md)).
- **Concentration risk under the backlog** — the same forward contracts lean heavily on a handful of buyers: roughly half of Oracle's backlog is tied to OpenAI, and Nscale's backlog is anchored by a single $45B Anthropic lease. Enormous visibility, narrow counterparty base.
- **Application layer earning its keep** — capital is also flowing to AI applications with real recurring revenue, not just to labs and compute: Harvey's $550M round at a $15.5B valuation on $400M+ ARR ([10 Sep](https://github.com/giovicordova/ai-finance-newsletter/blob/main/editions/2026-09-10.md)), rhyming with the Cognition AI raise noted [9 Sep](https://github.com/giovicordova/ai-finance-newsletter/blob/main/editions/2026-09-09.md).
