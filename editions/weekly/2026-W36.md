### Stories considered
- **Nvidia to acquire Hugging Face for ~$12.9 billion (CHOSEN)** — Nvidia confirmed on Thursday 3 Sep it will buy Hugging Face, the open-weight AI model-and-dataset platform, for about $12.93 billion: roughly $11.9 billion payable to shareholders plus up to $1 billion in retention equity for employees joining Nvidia. Expected to close in the first half of 2027, pending regulatory approval. It is Nvidia's second-largest acquisition on record (after ~$20B for Groq assets in December 2025) and a sharp step up from Hugging Face's $4.5B valuation in 2023; Hugging Face had turned down a $500M Nvidia investment last year that would have valued it at $7B. Nvidia committed to keep the platform open and support other silicon vendors. Chosen as the freshest, most consequential AI-finance beat of the cycle: the largest AI-software M&A of the week, extending the week's Nvidia-as-acquirer thread (MediaTek stake, 2 Sep) from silicon into the developer/model layer. Corroborated across CNBC, Yahoo Finance, CNN, Axios and The Hill; linked to CNBC (allowlisted, free).

### Pattern context
This closes a week in which Nvidia repeatedly moved from supplier to owner: a $3.5B convertible-bond stake in MediaTek (2 Sep) into the custom-silicon layer, and now ~$13B for Hugging Face into the model/developer layer. Alongside Broadcom's record $16.7B AI quarter (3 Sep) and Marvell's late-August data-centre surge, the demand side kept printing records — while the two macro/regulatory voices of the week (Fed Chair Warsh tying over half of 2026 US investment growth to AI on 31 Aug; the FSB's Bailey naming frontier AI the top systemic cyber risk on 1 Sep) reframed the same buildout as a force on rates and financial stability. The Hugging Face deal is the cleanest Friday capstone: consolidation is now reaching software, not just chips.
