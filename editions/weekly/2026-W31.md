### Stories considered
- **Amazon Q2 2026 results — chosen for the news post.** Released after the US close on Thursday 30 July 2026 via Amazon's investor-relations site (allowlisted company IR), Business Wire (press-release wire) and an SEC Form 8-K exhibit. Net sales rose 20% year-over-year to $200.6 billion (from $167.7 billion), Amazon's first quarter above $200 billion. Amazon Web Services, its cloud-computing arm, grew 36.7% to $42.2 billion — its fastest growth in 18 quarters (strongest since 2021) — with AWS operating income of $16.6 billion at a 39.4% margin and an annualized run rate near $169 billion. CEO Andy Jassy said the AI and custom-chips businesses each passed a $25 billion run rate. Company operating income rose 43% to $27.5 billion; net income was $62.6 billion, but that was inflated by ~$53.4 billion of non-operating pre-tax gains, primarily on its Anthropic stake. Capital spending kept climbing: purchases of property and equipment on a trailing-twelve-month basis reached ~$169 billion (up ~$66.1 billion / ~64% year-over-year), primarily AI infrastructure, and trailing-twelve-month free cash flow swung to an outflow of $7.6 billion. **This is the single most important AI-finance beat of the window** — the top-of-the-stack read that closes the week's build-out thread: the largest cloud provider's AI-driven demand accelerated to an 18-quarter high, even as its AI capital spending pushed free cash flow negative, echoing Thursday's Meta print. ### Pattern context
The channel has run the AI build-out story all week and for over two weeks before that. Monday's NAVER/NVIDIA/Brookfield up-to-$10B Korean data-centre plan set the financing frame; Tuesday's Celestica Q2 (+62%, raised forecast) and Wednesday's record SK hynix print (+257%, 76% margin, HBM4 mass shipments) were the demand and memory reads; Thursday's Meta report made the cost concrete, with $31B of quarterly AI spending cutting free cash flow ~91% to $784M, alongside Microsoft's Azure crossing $100B in annual revenue. Today's Amazon print is the top-of-the-stack close: AWS accelerated to an 18-quarter-high 37% even as trailing capex neared $169B and free cash flow turned negative. Markets doubted the spending for most of the week (a semiconductor-index bear market, the Nasdaq near a correction), then rerated the megacaps sharply on Thursday's prints.
