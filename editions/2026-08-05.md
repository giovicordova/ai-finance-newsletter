### Stories considered
- **AMD (Advanced Micro Devices) Q2 2026 results — chosen for the news post.** Reported after the US close on Tuesday 4 August 2026 via AMD Investor Relations (company IR, allowlisted, free), with corroborating free coverage on CNBC, Yahoo Finance, StockTitan and Investing.com. Total revenue was $11.5 billion, up 50% year-over-year and a company record, beating the ~$11.3 billion consensus. The data-centre segment (server chips and AI accelerators — EPYC processors and Instinct GPUs) reached $6.7 billion, up 107% year-over-year — more than doubled — and accounted for 58% of total revenue. Non-GAAP earnings per share were $1.66 (net income $2.8 billion), ahead of the ~$1.62 consensus; GAAP diluted EPS was $1.38 (net income $2.3 billion). AMD guided third-quarter revenue to $12.7–$13.3 billion (about 41% year-over-year growth at the midpoint), above the ~$12.5 billion consensus, and said it will begin shipping Helios, its first rack-scale AI system, to customers including Meta, OpenAI and Oracle this quarter. Despite the double beat, shares fell roughly 8% in after-hours trading on lofty expectations. **Chosen as the single most important AI-finance beat of the window** — the freshest hard, verifiable print, and the merchant-silicon (chips sold on the open market) leg flagged in yesterday's edition, extending the AI-demand thread from memory and software into the GPU/CPU supplier that competes most directly with Nvidia. ### Pattern context
The AI build-out thread the channel has tracked for three weeks keeps climbing the stack. It ran first on the supply side — memory (SK hynix, Kioxia), contract manufacturing (Celestica) and cloud (Amazon Web Services, Microsoft Azure) — then into applications software with Palantir's +149% U.S. commercial print yesterday. AMD is the merchant-silicon leg flagged in both Monday's and Tuesday's notes: the chipmaker that sells GPUs and server CPUs on the open market, the closest listed competitor to Nvidia. Its data-centre revenue more than doubling to $6.7 billion, plus first shipments of the Helios rack system to Meta, OpenAI and Oracle, says the same AI-data-centre demand feeding memory and software is now visibly reaching the compute layer beyond Nvidia. The tell to watch: AMD beat on revenue, earnings and guidance yet shares fell ~8% after hours — the recurring "beat-but-sell" reaction when expectations are already priced for perfection, the same doubt-then-rerate dynamic the channel logged around last week's megacap prints. Nvidia's own quarter later this month is the next test of whether the compute-demand story holds.
