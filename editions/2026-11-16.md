### Pattern context
The running spine remains "who pays for the AI buildout, at what valuation, and is the demand real." Last week ran the capital side (OpenAI's scaled-back $11B/$1.25T raise, 9 Nov), the physical-constraint side (Anthropic's $3.4B gas-turbine order, 10 Nov), the valuation peak (Anthropic's $70–$78 IPO pricing at up to ~$1.4T, 11 Nov), the demand proof (CoreWeave's near-tripled revenue and ~$133B backlog, 12 Nov) and the key-man/governance shock (Jensen Huang's resignation, 13 Nov). Today's beat is the demand side from a different vantage: Cisco — the picks-and-shovels networking supplier, not a pure-play AI landlord — raised its full-year revenue outlook and roughly doubled its hyperscaler AI-infrastructure revenue target to $9B, a second independent hard-number readout (after CoreWeave) that the orders behind the buildout are still landing. It sets the table for the week's real test: Nvidia reports tomorrow (17 Nov) — its first print without Huang — and Anthropic prices its IPO on 18 Nov. The recurring "the buyers, suppliers and financiers of AI are the same handful of names" thread holds: Cisco's AI revenue is hyperscaler spending, i.e. the same Microsoft/Amazon/Google/Meta capital flowing through every recent edition.
