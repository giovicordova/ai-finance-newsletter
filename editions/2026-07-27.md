### Stories considered
- **NAVER / NVIDIA / Brookfield up to $10 billion to expand Korea's national AI data centre — chosen for the news post.** Unveiled 24 July 2026 at an AI summit in San Francisco; joint press release issued 25 July via GlobeNewswire and PR Newswire (press-release wires, allowlisted, free). NAVER, NVIDIA and Brookfield announced an investment-and-financing plan of up to $10 billion to expand the NVIDIA DSX AI factory at NAVER's GAK Sejong data centre from 55 megawatts (announced last month) to 200 megawatts by 2028. Brookfield signed a non-binding letter of intent to provide up to $9 billion as exclusive capital partner; NVIDIA plans a $1 billion strategic investment in NAVER; NAVER funds the remainder. NAVER intends to expand the deployment to 1 gigawatt over time. Roles: NAVER operates, NVIDIA supplies the computing technology, Brookfield contributes long-term capital and power/infrastructure experience. **This is the single most important AI-finance beat of the window** — a discrete, dated, money-moving transaction that answers the channel's recurring "who funds the build-out?" question in a new way: a long-term infrastructure financier (Brookfield) fronting ~90% of a $10 billion AI data-centre bill, alongside a chip vendor taking an equity stake in its own customer. The news post links the GlobeNewswire primary and sticks to the four hard, verifiable facts (the $10B plan, Brookfield's up-to-$9B, NVIDIA's $1B stake, and the 55→200 MW by 2028 expansion). The 1 GW long-term target, the June 55 MW origin, the non-binding nature of Brookfield's LOI, and the sovereign-AI framing are held in these Notes to keep the post to headline facts. ### Pattern context
For over two weeks the channel has run the AI build-out story: chip suppliers (SK Hynix, ASML, TSMC), the software/data layer (Databricks' $188B mark), physical capacity (Hut 8's Beacon Point leases), and the compute layer's competitive structure (Alphabet's raised ~$205B 2026 capex, AMD's MI400/Helios launch on 24 July). Friday's weekly take flagged the open question as financing — "the market flinches at the bill," with 2026 AI capex estimated near $870B and rising hyperscaler debt. Today's NAVER/NVIDIA/Brookfield deal answers that question with a concrete new structure: a long-term infrastructure financier (Brookfield) fronting up to $9B of a $10B data-centre build, plus a chip vendor (NVIDIA) taking a $1B equity stake in its customer. The financing of the build-out — not just its scale — is now the live thread, and it is next tested by Microsoft and Meta earnings on 29 July and the Fed decision the same day.
