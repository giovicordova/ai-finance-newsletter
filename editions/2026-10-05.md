### Stories considered
- **Broadcom to lend Anthropic up to $42B, disclosed in Anthropic's IPO filing (CHOSEN).** First reported by Reuters on 1 Oct 2026 from Anthropic's IPO prospectus and widely syndicated through the weekend. Broadcom agreed to provide up to $42 billion in financing to Anthropic, roughly one-third of Anthropic's $125.2 billion five-year commitment to lease Broadcom's custom AI chips (tensor processing units). The financing takes the form of convertible notes that can later become Anthropic shares, and Broadcom has the option to bring in a financing partner. Broadcom is set to become Anthropic's largest compute supplier in 2027; the prospectus flags the dual supplier/lender relationship as a "potential conflict of interest." Chosen: the single biggest money-moving AI-finance structural event of the cycle, anchored in a primary document (Anthropic's own filing), and one this newsletter has not yet covered — Friday's beat was SoftBank completing its OpenAI investment. The post states only figures the filing supports and keeps the conflict-of-interest and IPO-timeline angles out of the factual beat.

### Pattern context
The weekend (3–4 Oct) was quiet, so today's beat reaches back to the biggest AI-finance item still uncovered here: Broadcom's up-to-$42B loan to Anthropic, disclosed in Anthropic's IPO filing. It extends the "debt underneath the buildout" thread the newsletter has tracked for two weeks — Anthropic's $11.6B Akamai compute deal (28 Sep), Sharon AI's GPU-backed debt (1 Oct), and SoftBank funding the final $10B of its OpenAI stake with a record bond sale (2 Oct). What's new and sharper here is the circularity: the chip supplier is also lending the customer the money to buy the chips, via notes that can convert into the customer's own equity. Bloomberg's report (2 Oct) that Broadcom's banks are amassing a $60B package — $42B senior-secured plus $18B junior led by Blackstone — shows how that commitment gets funded. The thread to watch is whether this supplier-as-lender structure becomes the template for the rest of the buildout, and how Anthropic's imminent public prospectus frames the conflict-of-interest risk.
