## Notes (archive only — not sent to Telegram)

### Stories considered
- **China confirms first US-China AI talks; trade truce extended to January 10 (CHOSEN).** Hours before Trump and Xi began their Washington summit on Thursday (24 Sep), China confirmed that the first US-China talks on artificial intelligence had taken place. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said the two sides discussed a formal AI dialogue and a mechanism to alert each other about AI incidents — specifically AI-related hacking that reaches a national-security level. Separately, Bessent said the "Busan Agreement" trade truce (struck by Trump and Xi in South Korea in late 2025, and due to expire ~10 Nov) was extended two months to 10 January, preserving lower tariffs (~20%) and China's resumed rare-earth/critical-mineral shipments. Chosen as the freshest, most consequential beat: it resolves this week's US-China AI-dialogue thread (opened 21 Sep) with a confirmed event, carries a money-moving trade dimension, and is verifiable on an allowlisted free source (CNBC) whose headline confirms both the AI talks and the truce extension. https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/24/china-confirms-first-ai-talks-with-us-have-taken-place-hints-at-trade-truce-extension.html
- **Xi urges US to cooperate on AI (runner-up — same thread, softer beat).** On Friday (25 Sep) Xi publicly urged the US to cooperate on AI, saying both countries have "the capability and responsibility" to keep AI "under human control." Fresher by a day but a rhetorical statement rather than a concrete outcome; the confirmed talks + truce extension is the harder beat. https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/25/chinas-xi-urges-us-to-cooperate-on-ai.html
- **Summit ends with no AI-safety agreement (runner-up — framing, not a hard event).** Multiple outlets noted the summit produced warm rhetoric but no guardrails agreement; Trump said he wants to "leave it exactly where it is." Useful for the weekly take's "what they left out" thread; too much interpretation for the factual news post.
- **AI-financing-cost / systemic-risk features (runner-up — paywalled/analytical).** Bloomberg ran pieces on rising AI data-centre financing costs, $33T of S&P 500 value tied to AI, and AI risk spreading across pension funds and bonds. Strong themes but paywalled (Bloomberg) and analytical rather than a 24h event beat; not linkable under the paywall rule.
- **Enterprise AI-in-finance product launches (Emburse AP, Abrigo Allowance) (runner-up — small).** Vendor product launches in AI accounts-payable and CECL automation; too small and promotional to lead.

### Pattern context
This week the spine was the US-China axis, read two ways. On chips: AMD (Advanced Micro Devices) topped $1 trillion (22 Sep) and Alibaba unveiled the Zhenwu V900, "China's most powerful" chip built to rival Nvidia amid US export curbs (23 Sep). On policy: a proposed US-China AI dialogue (21 Sep) that today's beat resolves into confirmed talks plus a trade-truce extension. Beneath both, private capital kept pooling (Tekever's $580M defense-AI raise, 24 Sep) while the 10-year Treasury yield hit a 2007 high. The through-line: the AI capital story is now an explicitly two-power buildout, and the two powers agreed to talk while leaving the hard levers — real AI guardrails and chip export controls — untouched.
