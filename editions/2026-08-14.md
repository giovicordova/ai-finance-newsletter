### Pattern context
August's dominant thread has been how the AI buildout is financed and what it costs, not whether demand is real — Nvidia organising $500 billion of Wall Street capital (11 Aug), CoreWeave's interest bill on its data-centre debt outrunning its losses (12 Aug), and Nebius spending about ten times its revenue in a single quarter (13 Aug). Applied Materials is the supply-side read on the same thread: the company selling the equipment that manufactures AI chips just posted record revenue up 25% and its highest sequential growth ever, and raised its outlook on "unprecedented" AI demand. Demand keeps confirming across the stack — chips, clouds, and now the tools that build the chips — while the cash going out to chase it, visible all week on the income statements, keeps arriving faster than profit.

### Weekly threads (Friday only)
- **Who pays for the buildout** — the week reframed the AI-infrastructure story from demand to financing: Nvidia lined up over $500 billion of third-party capital so customers can borrow against compute rather than pay upfront ([11 Aug](https://github.com/giovicordova/ai-finance-newsletter/blob/main/editions/2026-08-11.md)), setting the terms for everyone downstream.
- **Cost showing up on the income statement** — the two hard neocloud prints made the cost of that debt and capex visible: CoreWeave's net interest expense more than doubled to $640 million and now exceeds its net loss ([12 Aug](https://github.com/giovicordova/ai-finance-newsletter/blob/main/editions/2026-08-12.md)); Nebius spent $5.66 billion on chips and data centres — roughly ten times its revenue — and stayed in the red despite operating profit turning positive ([13 Aug](https://github.com/giovicordova/ai-finance-newsletter/blob/main/editions/2026-08-13.md)).
- **Supply side still setting records** — beneath the financing strain, demand kept confirming across the stack: AMD bought its way deeper into inference silicon ([10 Aug](https://github.com/giovicordova/ai-finance-newsletter/blob/main/editions/2026-08-10.md)) and chip-equipment maker Applied Materials posted record revenue up 25% with its highest-ever sequential growth on "unprecedented" AI demand (14 Aug).
