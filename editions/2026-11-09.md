### Pattern context
Last week's editions traced the AI-build-out spine through the real-revenue and chip-supply layers — Google Cloud +103% and Amazon Web Services +39% (prior week), Palantir's U.S. commercial revenue +148% (3 Nov), AMD's data-centre revenue +97% to a record $8.5B (4 Nov), Arm's data-centre royalties roughly doubling (5 Nov), and Microchip's PCIe Gen6 interconnect design wins doubling 12→24 (6 Nov). Friday's weekly take argued the market had stopped doubting the revenue and started doubting who pays for the buildout. Today's lead is that doubt made concrete at the top of the stack: the single largest AI buyer, OpenAI, closed a round that shrank from $30B+/$1.4T to ~$11B (first slice)/$1.25T as the early-November rout forced terms down — the clearest sign yet that the capital funding the buildout is getting more expensive to raise. The TeraWulf–Google–Anthropic backstop (the runner-up) is the same tension one layer down: counterparties are now engineering credit guarantees to get data-centre leases financed at all. Nvidia reports on 17 Nov — the next hard test of whether demand keeps outrunning the financing strain.
