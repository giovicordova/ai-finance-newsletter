### Stories considered
- **US Commerce Department (Bureau of Industry and Security) interim final rule tightening advanced-AI-chip export controls, issued 16 Nov 2026 [CHOSEN].** The Bureau of Industry and Security issued an interim final rule on 16 Nov 2026 requiring a US government licence before advanced AI chips and servers can be shipped to data centres outside the US that provide significant computing power to companies headquartered in restricted countries (China). The rule takes effect in 90 days; data centres already supplying significant compute to covered companies must obtain a licence within 90 days of the effective date to continue. It also designates Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Brazil as presenting an "unacceptable risk of diversion" (licence required for advanced-chip exports to them), treats advanced chips made for a Chinese firm as restricted at the foundry stage, and rescinds the Biden administration's January 2025 AI Diffusion Rule. BIS said the rule was written at the direction of the Treasury Department and National Security Council. Today's beat is the policy chokepoint: the US Commerce Department moved to license advanced-chip flows to overseas data centres that serve China-based firms, directly constraining the addressable market for the same Nvidia/AMD compute every recent edition has traced. It lands squarely between Nvidia's earnings (tonight) and Anthropic's IPO pricing (18 Nov) — a fitting bookend to a week that has moved from "is the demand real" to "how concentrated, how reversible, and now how regulated is the whole structure."
